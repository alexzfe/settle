# Designer intake attributes (researched 2026-09-13)

What professional interior designers record about a client's household, their Home, its Rooms, and the Items they keep. The sources are client questionnaires and site-survey checklists, listed at the end. This input is for the Home-model grilling ([handoff/home-model.md](../handoff/home-model.md)).

**How much to trust each source.** [1]–[3] are professional bodies: BIID, NKBA, and CIDQ, the body that runs the NCIDQ exam. [4]–[6] are practitioner and teaching material. [7]–[13] are trade blogs and vendor templates, used here only to show how often a question appears. No official intake form from ASID or IIDA is published openly. The standard textbooks (Piotrowski, Mitton & Nystuen) are not readable online.

**Prevalence** counts the sources listed against each attribute:
- **Universal:** in most sources of its kind, including at least one professional body.
- **Common:** in 2–3 sources.
- **Niche:** in one source, or only in renovation or kitchen material.

**Drives** gives the reason a source states. Where no source gives one, the reason is our own inference and is marked *(inf.)*.

## Headline

- **The survey's contents follow from the brief.** BIID: "when the design brief has been established, the information required from the survey should be apparent" [1]. Its baseline for an interior-design survey is dimensions, heights, service outlets and their heights, features, photos, existing furniture dimensions, and what has to be stored [1].
- **Understanding the user is the core activity.** In CIDQ's 2024 survey of 2,174 designers, "Determine User Needs" is the most frequent task in the programming phase: 3.55 on a scale where 3 is "fairly often" and 4 is "very often". "Evaluate Existing Building Conditions" scores 3.04 and carries the highest harm-if-wrong rating in that phase, 1.37 out of 2 [3].
- **The split is dimensional versus renovation.** A dimensional and photographic survey covers the attributes marked Universal below. Construction, services capacity, and hazardous materials appear only when works are planned [1][2].

**Tenure (rented or owned).** None of the forms I could verify asks "rent or own" as a bare field. Professional forms instead ask how long the client will stay [2] and what restricts changes (HOA, historic covenants) [2]. Renter guidance lists the usual lease bans: painting, wallpaper, nail or screw holes, permanent shelves, replacing blinds [13]. The advice then shifts to reversible, freestanding solutions [13].

The business-relationship questions are near-universal but irrelevant to design advice, so they are left out of the table. They cover referral source, preferred communication, decision speed, and who else decides [7][9][10].

The dimensional surveys [1][4][5] measure every opening and fixture by its distance along a wall, which puts it in the plan. None of them records finishes. Finishes appear only in the renovation form [2], and only as "use existing: yes/no".

## Consequences for us

- **BIID's list is the minimum for a Room.** It asks for wall lengths, ceiling height, openings, service outlets with their heights, features, photos, and the dimensions of existing Items [1]. Construction, services capacity, and hazards only matter for building work, so they can be optional for decorating and buying.
- **Measure along a wall, from a corner.** Every survey records a window, door, radiator, or socket as an offset along a wall, plus its size and height [4][5]. Recording each one as "which wall + offset + size + height" in v1 extends straight into a 2D Floor Plan.
- **Sill height, door swing, and outlet positions directly change advice** [1][4]. The other surveyed attributes mostly feed drawing accuracy.
- **Tenure is best recorded as restrictions plus length of stay.** A bare rented/owned flag is not what the sources collect. Lease and HOA restrictions map naturally to Constraints [2][13]. How long the user plans to stay feeds the Budget [2][9].
- **Most household facts map onto existing CONTEXT.md terms.** Occupants, pets, and accessibility needs are candidate Constraints, but only once the user confirms them. Pain points and hobbies are Session input or Notes. Style, colour, and material preferences belong to the Design Direction. Budget range and splurge/save areas belong to the Budget module.
- **An Item needs more than a keep/replace flag.** The sources also record what happens to a kept piece (as-is, refinish, reupholster, move to another Room), its condition, a photo, and its seat height [11]. The "move to another Room" option means an Item's Room can change.
- **Delivery access is rarely collected but matters for buying.** Door widths are always measured [2][4][5], but whether something fits along the route into a Room appears only in [2]. For Purchase Decisions it is a cheap *must* Requirement *(inf.)*.

## Gaps

- The CIDQ blueprints give only task-level headings. Their detailed knowledge statements were not retrievable.
- No verified form asks "rent or own" as a bare question. A Scribd template reportedly asks about "ownership status", but I did not read it.
- How orientation, latitude, and obstructions affect colour and lighting is left to the separate daylight research brief.
